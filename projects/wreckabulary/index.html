The problem

Word games usually mean sitting still and staring at tiles, while party brawlers rarely ask you to think. Wreckabulary asks what happens when spelling is the weapon: in a cozy world where every object is built from its own letters, smashing the room hands you the vocabulary to fight back.

What I built

So far I've designed and built the game on my own; the rest of the team joins for later milestones.

  • Set up the Unity 6 (URP) project and the first playable Dibs! prototype
  • Wrote the game design doc and roadmap: the premise, the house hub, the modes and the maps
  • Built the house hub with its typewriter mode select, and a 7-step tutorial
  • Built all four modes: Dibs! and Furnish First (versus), Moving Day (co-op, 3 levels) and Home Sweet Home (a creative sandbox with rooms saved as JSON)
  • Built the spelling system: letter-by-letter spelling with hints and undo, dropping letters to make room, carrying and throwing
  • Designed 43 objects built Word World-style from their own letters, across living room, bedroom, kitchen and garden arenas
  • Synthesised every sound effect and music track in code, and added a wardrobe of cosmetics (halo, tiara, chef hat, tie, cape)

How it works

  • Smash
  • Scavenge
  • Spell
  • Summon

Smash furniture and it bursts into the letters that spell it. Walk over letters to carry up to six, then spell a word letter by letter over your head. Real words are cast instantly: action words like BLADE, WINGS or BEES do something, and object words build the thing Word World-style, like a SOFA with S and A armrests. Getting hit knocks letters loose, so everything you summon can be smashed and re-spelled.

Everything starts in the hub, a house friends join by walking in the front door. A typewriter picks the mode: Dibs! (versus), Furnish First (versus), Moving Day (co-op) or Home Sweet Home (a creative sandbox with rooms saved as JSON).

One hard problem

Making objects out of their own letters

Every object in Wreckabulary is built from the letters that spell it: a SOFA has S and A armrests, an O backrest and an F cushion. That only works if each of the 43 objects still reads as the thing it is, comes out at a sensible size, and doesn't topple over the moment it lands, because every one of them is a physics object that gets grabbed, thrown and smashed.

Instead of hand-placing letters for each object, I turned every object into a small recipe in LetterShapes: each letter gets a centre, a size, whether it stands up or lies flat, and optionally its own colour (a brown trunk under green leaves). The game builds the object from its recipe at runtime, and FurnitureCatalog gives it a weight and colour. To stop 43 designs from quietly breaking each other, play-mode tests build every object word and check that it comes out at a sensible size and stays standing.
